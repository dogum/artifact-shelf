    Why it reads as clouds
    
  
    Nothing in the code knows about clouds. Nineteen rounds of abs(), a scale and an offset build a kaleidoscopic IFS fractal, and the 7.1/dot(p,p) term is a sphere inversion, which turns the flat folds into rounded, billowing lobes at every scale. The camera is orthographic, tilted about 63° so the fractal's "up" lands at the top of the frame.
    
  
    The color trick is the clever part. Each step subtracts a faint orange. Where the ray passes through dense fractal structure, little gets subtracted and the pixel stays near white. Where it passes through nothing, 98 subtractions of orange leave you with its complement, sky blue. Translucency, lighting and sky come from one line of arithmetic.
    

  
    What the rebuild does instead
    
  
    The right side is a conventional real-time cloud renderer, the kind used in games, with everything the one-liner fakes done explicitly.

    IFS fractal as density
    A 128³ Perlin–Worley volume baked on the GPU at load, eroded by a Worley detail channel, shaped by a two-scale weather map and a cumulus height profile. Mipmapped and sampled at each step's footprint, so distant cloud doesn't shimmer.
    
    
    Orthographic camera on a fixed tilt
    Perspective camera on a planet of radius 6360 km. Clouds fill a shell from 1.5 to 4.2 km; Altitude flies you from the ground up through the deck and out above it.
    
    
    Color by subtraction
    Beer–Lambert transmittance, four octaves of approximate multiple scattering with a Henyey–Greenstein phase, a six-sample light march, and sky light averaged over the hemisphere.
    
    
    Sky implied
    Rayleigh, Mie and ozone single scattering, with aerial perspective applied to both clouds and ground. The sun reddens as it drops and the clouds follow.
    
    
    No ground
    Fields and forest from the same noise, with cloud shadows that drift with the wind.
    
    
    98 steps, every frame from scratch
    About 40 jittered steps per frame, blended with the previous frames after reprojecting them, so a still view converges to hundreds of samples per pixel.
    
    
    Animation: axis wobble
    Wind carries the volume; the detail noise drifts slowly so the edges boil.

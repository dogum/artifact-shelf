MERROW

A pocket plan in thirty-two panels. Somewhere in its folds is a walk the city forgot.

FOLDED AS BOUGHT

Drag the cover downward to open it panel by panel, or let it fall open.

HOW TO FOLD

  • Press on a panel and drag it across one of the scored creases. Let go past halfway and it folds.
  • Valley brings the flap over toward you; mountain sends it behind. Press V or M, or hold Shift while dragging to swap.
  • You lift what is on top. A valley fold carries everything lying on the flap; a mountain fold carries everything under it. Paper never passes through paper.
  • Turn over (T) to read the reverse. Turn around (R) spins the sheet on the desk.
  • Z undoes a fold. Creases stay in the paper after you undo them, as they would.
  • Scroll or pinch to zoom. Drag the desk to slide the map. Right-drag to tilt and look along the folds.

Lower the lamp to read the creases by raking light.

CARTOGRAPHER’S KEY

Hints open one at a time. The answer is below them.

The legend lists one symbol you never see whole on the open plan. The street index on the reverse still gives it four squares.

The rhyme in the corner names five lanes. Find each one. Every one of them meets a street that runs exactly along a crease.

Two boundaries run dead straight along the scored lines. The reverse explains how the printers mark a fold, and the legend draws the boundaries the same way.

Four quarters of one round court sit at four junctions in four parishes. Make them touch.

The answer

Pleat out columns D and E: mountain along the parish line (C|D), valley along the ward line (D|E). Then pleat out rows 2 and 3 the same way: mountain along the parish line under North Parade, valley along the river. Half the city disappears, and the round runs from Holloway & Pike’s door through Wick Lane, Tallow Passage, Taper Row, Chandlers Mews and Ember Steps to Lantern Court.

The false lead

The Lantern on the open plan is a pub. Pleat out columns C and D and Wick Lane runs straight on into Gaslight Row and Mantle Lane, and the pub’s half-round closes up with a half-round beer garden from across the sheet. A perfect circle at the end of a perfect street. The rhyme never mentions gas.

How the page knows

After every fold it checks the real stack of paper: every point along the round, and around the court, has to be face up, on top, and laid out on the desk in one piece at the right spacing. Any sequence of folds that achieves that counts.

The Lantern, found

Wick Lane, Tallow Passage, Taper Row, Chandlers Mews, Ember Steps: the lamplighters’ round runs again and ends at Lantern Court, where the four parishes met. On the open plan none of these lanes touch.

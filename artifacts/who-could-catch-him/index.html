The odds board

    Scoring by age

    How a scorer's goals per game change from one season to the next at each age, measured on every pair of seasons since 1979 (the delta method). Scorers peak around 23 and lose about 9% a year by 30.

    Staying in the league

    The chance that a player who scored at least 0.25 goals a game is back in the NHL the next season. It holds near 95% until the mid-30s, then falls.

    Games played

    How much of the next season those scorers actually play. Injuries are drawn from these real seasons, so some simulated years are cut short.

    Does it work?

    The model

    Each career starts from the player's last three seasons (weighted 3:2:1, shrunk toward 0.30 goals a game by 140 games of evidence). Each future season he either leaves the league, or his scoring rate moves by the aging factor for his age plus a random shift, he plays a real-season share of 82 games, and his goals are a Poisson draw from that rate.

    What it leaves out

    Lockouts, rule changes, trades and contracts. A player who misses a full season counts as leaving. Goalies are excluded. Ovechkin's own finish is simulated the same way for the rest of this season, and he retires after it, as he has announced.
